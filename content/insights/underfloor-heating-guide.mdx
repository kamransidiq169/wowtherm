## Why Underfloor Heating?

Underfloor heating is one of the most efficient and comfortable ways to heat a space. Unlike radiators that heat air near the ceiling, radiant floor heating warms the room from the ground up — exactly where you feel it.

The result is even temperature distribution, lower energy consumption, and complete freedom in interior design. No radiators on walls, no vents in floors, no visible hardware whatsoever.

## How It Works

There are two primary types of underfloor heating:

### Electric Systems

Electric heating mats or cables are installed beneath the floor finish. When electricity flows through the elements, they generate heat that radiates upward through the floor.

Best for: Renovations, individual rooms, bathrooms, quick installation.

### Hydronic (Water-Based) Systems

Warm water flows through PEX or PERT pipes embedded in the floor. Connected to a boiler or heat pump, the entire floor becomes a low-temperature radiator.

Best for: New builds, whole-home heating, lowest running costs.

## Choosing the Right System

The choice between electric and hydronic depends on several factors:

- **Floor type**: Tile and stone work with both systems. Laminate and engineered wood need specific solutions.
- **Room size**: Electric is cost-effective for smaller spaces. Hydronic becomes more economical for larger areas.
- **Energy source**: If you have a heat pump, hydronic is the natural choice.
- **Installation**: Electric is simpler to install. Hydronic requires more planning but delivers lower running costs.

## The Importance of Insulation

Regardless of which heating system you choose, proper insulation beneath the heating elements is critical. Without it, heat flows downward into the subfloor instead of upward into the room.

Quality insulation boards can reduce heat-up times by 50% and cut running costs by up to half. This is the single most cost-effective upgrade in any underfloor heating installation.

## Smart Controls

Modern thermostats do more than just set temperature. They learn your routine, adapt to occupancy patterns, and optimize energy usage automatically.

Features to look for:
- Dual air and floor temperature sensing
- Wi-Fi connectivity and app control
- Geofencing for automatic away modes
- Energy reporting and consumption tracking
- Integration with smart home systems
